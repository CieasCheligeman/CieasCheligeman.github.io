Magnitude is the size of the earthquake; what you feel is the shaking where you are. The same M7 is a violent jolt directly overhead and a slow sway 300 km away. So choose a magnitude, then how far away you are, how deep it breaks and what ground you stand on. The simulator estimates the Japanese intensity, 震度 (shindo), you would experience, and plays it out in a room.

Every magnitude

Each step ≈ 32× the energy Frequencies: USGS, worldwide

Why the numbers climb so steeply. Each whole step in magnitude releases about 32 times more energy, so an M9 releases roughly a billion times the energy of an M3. Bigger earthquakes also break a longer stretch of fault. That's why the strong shaking lasts so much longer and reaches so much further, not just why it's stronger.

    04

    When it really shakes

    What to do
    1. Protect your head first. Get under a sturdy table and hold on to its legs, or cover your head with a cushion or bag. Drop, cover, hold on.
    2. Keep away from what can fall. Windows, shelves, tall furniture, hanging lights.
    3. Don't run outside while it shakes. Roof tiles, glass and signs fall into the street.
    4. When it stops, open a door so it can't jam shut, put shoes on against broken glass, and expect aftershocks.
    5. Near the coast, go to high ground at once after strong or long shaking. Don't wait for a tsunami warning.

    Japan's early warning buys seconds. The first waves to arrive (P-waves) are fast but weak; the damaging S-waves follow. The Earthquake Early Warning (緊急地震速報) uses that gap. The simulator shows it too: the further away you set the epicentre, the longer the pause between the first jolt and the main shaking.

    For official guidance, follow the Japan Meteorological Agency and your local authority.

    This is an illustration, not a prediction. The intensity comes from a published ground-motion model for Japan (peak ground velocity after Si & Midorikawa, 1999, converted to JMA instrumental intensity after Fujimoto & Midorikawa, 2005), with a simple factor for the ground under you. Below M5.5, outside the data the relation was fitted to, a small correction brings it in line with typical JMA reports. Real shaking varies a lot with fault geometry, direction and local soil, so the same earthquake can differ by a whole intensity level between neighbouring streets. The waveform is synthetic, and the room responds at the thresholds in JMA's intensity descriptions.
